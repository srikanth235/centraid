Centraid · the personal superapp

Own your data. The apps only borrow it.

Photos, documents, notes, calendar, tasks, people, passwords, shared expenses — all of it one record in a vault on your own disk. Eight apps that work like the ones you already use read and write it, so your calendar knows your people and your spending knows its receipts. Delete an app and you lose nothing of yours; leaving is copying a folder.

Why the record comes firstin five chapters

01 one record

Apps that finally know each other.

Under the eight apps sits one record on one shared model: a person, a payment, a plan, a picture is declared exactly once, and every app draws on the same rows. The friend in People is the birthday in Agenda, the face in Photos, the balance in Tally — one row, not four copies drifting apart.

So cross-app life just happens: birthdays ride your calendar without a second calendar, due tasks fold into your day, a checklist line in Notes becomes a real task in one gesture — linked back, never copied. Star something once and every app knows.

02 your vault

A record you can hold in your hand.

It all lives in one vault on your disk — files you can see, copy and walk away with. Export is honest: copy a directory. No export request form, no 30-day download link, no format held hostage.

Apps borrow the record through scoped grants you sign — installing one is a sentence you can read, not a shrug — every access leaves a receipt in an append-only journal, and secrets stay sealed even at rest.

03 the assistant

Ask one place about everything.

The point of one record is that something can finally think across it. The assistant reads your whole vault — receipted, row-capped — and answers what no siloed app could, because your people, money, calendar and documents were never separated in the first place.

It acts only through the same typed commands your apps use — consequential acts pause for your confirmation, and when you correct it, the correction becomes a durable rule it consults next time.

04 your people

Sharing is a grant, not an upload.

Sharing is a sentence you sign: this person may view this album, until I say otherwise. A new photo reaches them with no second gesture, an edit follows, and revoking asks their vault to remove its copy — delivered vault to vault, no cloud in the middle.

A household shares one machine without sharing one identity: each person gets a sovereign vault — not a sub-account — and what crosses between them is exactly what was granted. A Tally group is the same idea for money: everyone adds expenses, every member's own copy derives the same balances.

05 your devices

One gateway. Everything else is a client.

There is no sync service because there is nothing to sync: one gateway holds the vault, and your laptop and phone are clients in a star around it, connected by direct, end-to-end-encrypted peer-to-peer links (iroh) — no relay to rent, no account to lose.

Offline is designed, not accidental: your phone reads its own local replica, and a write made in a tunnel queues and lands exactly once when you're back. Pairing is one QR code — a ticket that burns on use — and revocation cuts live connections, not just future ones.

Where it runsone gateway · three homes

It runs where your life runs.

local-first: the vault is files on your machine, the gateway is the only door to it, and nothing leaves your devices unless you point it somewhere.

home 1

Desktop shell

The gateway embedded in an app on your Mac, Linux, or Windows machine. Open it like anything else.

home 2

Always-on daemon

centraid-gateway serve on a spare machine or home server — your apps and automations keep working with the laptop closed.

home 3

Phone in your pocket

The mobile companion is a thin client onto any gateway you run. Same apps, same data, no sync service.

→ get started

One clone to your own vault.

# macOS · Linux · Windows — needs Bun ≥ 1.3 and Node ≥ 24
$ git clone https://github.com/srikanth235/centraid
$ cd centraid && bun install
$ bun run dev:desktop
# a spare machine or home server — devices pair to this
$ bun run build
$ centraid-gateway serve --data-dir ~/centraid-data
$ centraid-gateway pair --data-dir ~/centraid-data --vault Local

First launch asks your name, mints your vault, and lands on the app grid. Installing an app from Discover is a grant you can read, not a copy — uninstall any time and your data stays.

Ten minutes in you can pair your phone with one QR code and ask the assistant your first cross-silo question. The start guide walks the whole first day.

Your record will outlive every app that ever held a piece of it. Keep it somewhere you own.
