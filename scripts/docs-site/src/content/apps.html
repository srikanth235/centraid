Chapter 2 · apps

Nine apps. One phone vault.

Centraid ships its nine first-party apps together. Open any of them from Home: each is a native screen over the same personal vault on this phone. The apps are reviewed parts of one product, not separate principals with access to request or revoke.

§01 · the apps

Different work, shared records.

Docs keeps files you own, with folders, editable text documents and version history. Photos keeps imported photographs, albums, places and hand-added links to people. Notes is for writing, notebooks and links to other records. People keeps your people and their contact details, relationships and the records linked to them. Locker holds logins, cards and other sensitive items behind the phone's unlock check.

Calendar shows your events and can show reminders on their due days. Reminders holds tasks and lists; completing a reminder from Calendar uses Reminders' own action. Money organizes statement lines you import and derives totals from those records. Health follows supported data from the phone's health store and also lets you log daily figures and workouts by hand. Its health-store connection reads only; it never writes back to that store. These are the nine apps in Home; there is no app catalogue or install step.

The apps use shared vault rules for search, links, attachments, trash and record history. Eligible records can link to one another, and records can hold a file or photo; Locker items have no attachments and their links are visible only in Locker. A record says how it arrived: typed, imported from a file, or followed from a phone store. A real example starts when you add a file in Docs, link a note to that document, then link a reminder to the note. Each step uses the app's existing add or link action; the records remain one set of records in the vault. Centraid does not add sample records to your vault.

one safe change rule

Trash, history and Undo

Records can be moved to the shared trash and restored before they are purged. Editable primary records keep earlier values, and the status line offers Undo for an undoable action. A permanent delete or empty-trash action is confirmed. A few kinds of data have different ownership: for example, a day's Health total has no trash, and a trashed imported Money line leaves totals and search until restored.

take your data out

Export and share

More → Export is the common entry to each app's export; Export everything includes every app except Locker. Exporting plaintext or sharing a primary record asks for the phone's owner check first. A phone without a passcode warns and continues; Locker export refuses. Primary records share as copies from their detail screen: Docs shares the stored file, Photos shares the original, and other apps share a small file. Locker items cannot be shared; Locker's own confirmed export writes to the system save sheet.
