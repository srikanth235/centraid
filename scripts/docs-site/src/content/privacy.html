Privacy policy · effective 15 October 2026

Your phone keeps the vault and its keys.

The Centraid phone app has no account. Your phone keeps the live vault; a gateway you choose receives only sealed backup data. Recovery needs your 24-word key and a reachable sealed backup.

Where information goes

On this phone

The vault, imported records, assistant chats and locally run assistant stay in the phone app.

To a gateway

The phone seals backup data before sending it directly. The gateway cannot open the copy.

The phone and its permissions

The vault is a database in the phone app's private storage. The database file itself is not encrypted; the phone's lock and storage protections guard it. Locker secrets are encrypted inside the vault and open only after you unlock Locker. The Android app opts out of operating-system backup. On iPhone, Centraid marks vault files for exclusion from iCloud backup.

Centraid asks the operating system for access when you choose an import or connect a source. You can refuse. Turning a permission off stops future reads; records already imported stay in the vault until you delete them.

  • Photos: reads items you allow from the phone's photo library, including available date and place details. Originals that exist only in iCloud are fetched only when the backup rule allows it.
  • Contacts: imports names and available contact details. Following can read later changes; it does not write changes back to Contacts.
  • Calendars: reads events from calendars you choose, not the Birthdays calendar. Following can read later changes; it does not write to the calendar.
  • Reminders: on iPhone, reads open reminders and available notes and due dates. It does not write back.
  • Health: when you tap Connect, reads the health records you allow from Apple Health or Android Health Connect. Centraid does not write to those stores.
  • Camera and files: the camera scans pairing QR codes and captures document scans; the file picker opens files you choose. The operating system presents those choices.

Face ID, fingerprint or your passcode unlocks Locker through the phone. Centraid receives the result, not your biometric data.

The assistant runs on your phone

The on-device model reads the vault data it needs to answer. Your question, answer and the data it reads stay on the phone. It cannot read Locker secrets, and it changes nothing until you confirm an action. Chats are saved in the vault and go with its backup.

When you choose Download model, the phone fetches the release's model file from Hugging Face. The first time you attach a photo to a chat, it may fetch a separate vision file there. These requests carry no vault data; the service can receive ordinary connection details such as your network address. The hosting provider's terms apply.

Backups and the recovery key

Your phone seals the vault and any backup files before sending them directly over HTTPS to the gateways you pair. It pins each gateway's certificate from its pairing QR. A gateway can see connection details, the phone label, data sizes and timing, but has no key to read the backup. There is no Centraid account or relay in this transfer.

The 24 words of your recovery key derive the keys that open the backup. When available, iCloud Keychain on iPhone or Google's Block Store on Android may keep a protected copy. Android returns that copy only during new-phone setup. The Backup screen says where the key is saved and lets you keep it off that service. Keep the words, and keep a reachable gateway with a sealed backup.

Backups keep recent daily, weekly and monthly snapshots, so deleted records can remain in older copies. The phone requests deletion only after no kept snapshot needs an item. The gateway holds a requested deletion for at least seven days, and may keep it longer while paused or offline. See backup retention.

Your choices and deletion

Manage photo, contact, calendar, reminder, health, camera, notification and local-network permissions in the phone's Settings. Imported records remain in the vault if you revoke permission.

Forget a gateway in Backup to stop this phone sending to it. If it is unreachable, revoke its token with centraid-gateway pairings revoke. Copies already stored remain until their backup retention expires or the operator removes the data.

Delete an item in Centraid to remove it from the vault; some apps keep deleted items in Trash first. Older backups retain data while a kept snapshot needs it. Deleting the app removes the local vault, but a recovery-key copy may remain with the platform and gateways may still hold backups.

Centraid keeps no account record to export, correct or delete on request. Questions about privacy or deletion: srikanth@crowdshakti.com.
