Privacy policy · effective 15 October 2026

Your phone keeps the keys.

Centraid keeps your vault on your phone. Encrypted copies go only to a backup device you own, which cannot read them. Centraid has no account and no cloud, and runs no server that holds your data.

Centraid collects nothing about you. It contains no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting. The sections below say what the app reads on this phone, what the phone sends and fetches, and where it goes.

§02 · on this phone

The vault is a file on this phone.

Everything you add to Centraid's apps lives in your vault, a database on this phone that only Centraid's code on this phone writes. Centraid does not encrypt the vault file itself: the phone's lock and storage protection guard it. Locker secrets are the exception. They are encrypted inside the vault and open only after you unlock Locker. Centraid also marks its files so that the phone's iCloud or Google backup leaves them out.

What Centraid reads from this phone. When you make a vault, the phone asks, one at a time, whether Centraid may read your photos, contacts, calendars and, on iPhone, reminders. Each answer is yours, and Centraid reads nothing you decline. If you decline, the apps offer the import again later.

Photos and videos are read from your library, with the place each was taken, to fill Photos and to back them up. Centraid looks for new ones each time it opens and when a backup runs, until you turn photo access off in Settings. Contacts bring names, phone numbers, email addresses and birthdays. Calendars bring events from your editable calendars, from the past week to the next two months, with titles, notes, times and repeat rules. Reminders bring open reminders with their notes and due dates. Contacts, calendars and reminders are copied into the vault once, and nothing is written back.

Camera and unlock. The camera scans the code on a backup device and scans paper into Docs. Those images stay on this phone. Face ID, fingerprint or your passcode unlock Locker. The phone does that check, and Centraid never receives it.

§03 · the assistant

It runs on this phone.

When you ask the assistant something, a small language model on this phone reads what it needs from your vault and writes the answer. Your questions, its answers and what it reads never leave the phone. It never reads Locker, and it changes nothing until you confirm.

Chats are saved in your vault, backed up with it, and deleted when you delete them. The model is a file of roughly half a gigabyte that Centraid downloads once, when you tap Download model, and a smaller second file the first time you attach a photo to a chat. Both come from huggingface.co. The request carries no vault data. Hugging Face sees what any download shows, such as your network address, and its own terms apply.

§04 · backups and the recovery key

A copy only your key opens.

A backup device is a computer you own or rent that runs Centraid's backup software, centraid-gateway. You pair it by scanning the QR code it shows. This phone then encrypts your vault, photos and files and sends them straight to the addresses in that code, over a connection it checks against the backup device's own certificate. Nothing sits in between: no relay and no Centraid service. The backup device stores what arrives. It holds no key, so it cannot open any of it. Installing the software downloads Centraid's release from github.com.

What a backup device can see. The name you gave it for your phone, when and from where the phone connects, and how much is stored and when. Nothing hides sizes or timing. It cannot read the contents, and neither can anyone who takes its files.

Old copies and off-site copies. Backups keep recent daily, weekly and monthly copies, so a note or photo you delete can stay in older copies for months. You own the backup device, so deleting its data deletes every copy. One backup device at home is a local backup: fire or theft can take the phone and the backup device together. A second backup device somewhere else is the off-site copy.

Your recovery key is the only way back. Centraid makes your recovery key, 24 words, when you make your first vault, and every key that protects your vault comes from it. If you lose this phone and the recovery key, the vault is gone. Centraid cannot reset or recover the key, because it never has a copy. To restore on a new phone, type the 24 words of your recovery key and pair the backup device.

Where the key is kept. This phone keeps the key in its protected storage, and the Backup tab shows it again after the phone's own check. By default the phone's system also saves a copy in iCloud Keychain on iPhone, or with Google on Android, so a new phone can bring it back. That copy is Apple's or Google's to keep under their terms, and Centraid never receives it. Turn it off in the Backup tab, under Recovery key, and the key stays on this phone alone.

§06 · your choices

Turn it off, forget it, delete it.

Permissions. Photos, contacts, calendars, reminders, camera and local network are the phone's to give and take back, in Settings. Centraid stops reading the moment you turn one off. What it already copied stays in your vault until you delete it.

Backup devices. Forget one in the Backup tab and this phone stops sending to it and tells it to refuse this phone. If it cannot be reached, run centraid-gateway pairings revoke on it. Copies already stored stay there until you delete them. Backup settings choose whether videos are included and whether backups use Wi-Fi or cellular.

Deleting. Delete an item, a photo or a chat and it leaves the vault. Some apps keep deleted items in a trash for a while first, and older backups keep them until they age out. Deleting the app removes the vault from this phone. The phone's keychain can still hold your recovery key afterward, so a reinstall can find it, and backup devices keep their copies.

Nothing to request. Centraid holds no record of you, so there is nothing for it to export, correct or delete on request. If you think otherwise, write to the address below.
