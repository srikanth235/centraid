# Mobile offline data

> **What is current, and what is a record.** The phone is the vault ([#1029](https://github.com/srikanth235/centraid/issues/1029)) and its backup is the plane [#1080](https://github.com/srikanth235/centraid/issues/1080) rebuilt: where the phone's files live, what a pass does in the foreground and the background, the member's transfer rule, the phone's disk, and what Centraid may tell a member per app state. The seat plane #1029 deleted (a replica per device, its log tail, its outbox and its bootstrap) is gone from this document. How the phone's screens read is [mobile/README.md](../mobile/README.md#the-kit-the-app-queries-and-adding-an-app-screen).

**The phone works offline because there is nothing to be offline from.** It holds `vault.db` and is its only writer; every read and every write is local, and no screen waits on a network. What needs a network is the **backup**: sealed copies of the vault and of every original and derivative it knows, uploaded to the gateways the member paired, by a **pass** that the shell runs in the foreground and in the windows the platform grants ([docs/gateway.md](gateway.md), [docs/recovery/backup-restore.md](recovery/backup-restore.md)).

## Durable path and at-rest decision

A device's vault files live in the app's own data directory — Android's `filesDir`, iOS's Documents directory (`ShellModel.vaultDirectory`) — and the directory is the roster: every `<name>/vault.db` in it is a vault this device holds, one directory per vault so each has its own backup home ([R-1047-E8](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)); a new one is founded in `centraid-vault-<random>/vault.db` (`Shelf`, `mobile/shared/.../shell/Shelf.kt`). Beside each `vault.db`:

| Path | What it holds | Lose it and |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `vault.bytes/` | The content store: files named by their BLAKE3 — every derivative, and every original with no home in the operating system's library (an edit, a document, a download, an original fetched back from a gateway) | the originals only it held are gone unless a gateway confirmed them |
| `vault.backup.db` | The **ledger**: the paired gateways (addresses, pinned certificate, token, epoch), the queue of parts to move, every name each gateway confirmed, the snapshots this phone took, and where each content hash's bytes are on this phone — the operating system's library, the content store, or nowhere | the phone pairs again and reconciles; nothing a gateway holds is lost |
| `vault.spool/` | **Sealed parts waiting to move**, one file per name, at most 2 GiB or a tenth of the free space, whichever is smaller | the next pass seals again |
| `vault.keep-originals.json` | The albums kept out of **Free up space** ([R-1029-PH-2](decisions.md#photos)) | every album reads as not kept |

**The phone does not copy its photo library in.** A photograph or a video in the operating system's library is read where it lives and streamed once through hash and seal into the spool; the ledger records that its bytes are the library's, and the grid draws its thumbnail from the content store ([R-1080-6](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)). An original larger than the room the spool has is hashed on that first read and sealed on later ones, a window of parts per read, under the names the first read made known ([R-1080-C39](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)): one more read per window, instead of an original that never backs up.

The ledger, the spool and the keep list are **device-local and derived, never inside the vault**: a snapshot of the vault never carries the state of its own upload, and a restored phone must not inherit a dead phone's facts about its own disk. Android excludes all of the app's data from Auto Backup and device-to-device transfer (`android:allowBackup="false"` plus the data-extraction rules in `mobile/androidApp/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml`); iOS excludes every vault-derived path from the OS backup by name, because exclusion does not inherit ([R-1029-8](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)).

These files survive OS cache eviction and disappear on uninstall. Centraid does not add SQLCipher or field encryption to the vault file beyond its declared sealed columns: that keeps the phone's own SQLite/FTS read plane and its cold/search budgets, and relies on the platform's at-rest encryption. A future stronger scheme must preserve on-device search and publish measured costs before replacing this decision.

Locker is stricter in ONE direction: the secret half. The phone is the unlock boundary and the only place the vault's Locker key becomes plaintext ([`crates/core/src/locker`](../crates/core/src/locker/mod.rs)), behind the phone's own biometric or passcode ([D-5](decisions.md#locker)); in the file a secret value is `lk1:` ciphertext under `K` ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md#at-rest-formats)).

## Background work and push privacy

### The pass

**One mechanism backs a vault up: the pass** — `drain` in the core, `DrainPass` and `ShelfDrain.run(deadlineMs, reason)` in the shared module. It runs in three steps, each one durable before the next, so a pass cut off at any point resumes from the ledger and the spool rather than from the beginning:

1. **Prepare.** Take a snapshot if one is due — hourly while a gateway is reachable on an unmetered link, at once at a gateway this phone has set no head at (one just paired, or paired again), and whenever the pass's reason wants one (**Back up now**, the app leaving the screen, a restore that just finished; a request the pass could not meet because no gateway answered is kept, and the next pass that reaches one takes it) — then ask `exists` and seal into the spool only what no gateway has confirmed: the snapshot's changed ranges and its manifest, and every original and derivative the vault knows by hash — a file in the trash until its window runs out, a file deleted forever not at all — newest first, under the member's rule and up to the spool's budget. A body the vault keeps in its own row — a note, a text document, a message — is not one of them: its bytes are the vault file's, so it is backed up with the records that hold it, and the status counts it with them rather than as a file. The `exists` question covers every file the member backs up, whatever the rule lets this pass seal — asking moves no bytes — so a restored phone learns on its first pass what its gateway already holds. The core seals what its own store holds; for an original that lives in the operating system's library it answers `need_bytes`, and the shell streams those bytes through the stage door, which hashes and seals in the same stream. The hash of an item a pass asks for is already known, so the pass plans which of its parts the spool has room for — every item that fits whole, then as many parts of the first larger one as the rest leave — and the stage door seals those under their names, keeping them only if what streamed hashes to the item; a read of an item edited since its first is dropped. A pass that moved a window asks for the next one before it answers.
2. **Move.** Upload the spool to the preferred reachable gateway — the LAN one when home — checking the deadline between parts. Android moves every part inside the pass. iOS moves what it can while the app is alive and **hands the rest to the system's background `URLSession`**: `handoff` gives the shell a batch of spool files with their URLs and headers, the operating system uploads them while the app is suspended, and each part carries whether it may cross a metered link, so the member's rule holds for uploads the OS runs on its own ([R-1080-2](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)). Before a batch is handed off the shell asks `reconcile`, and nothing is handed to a gateway that did not answer.
3. **Settle.** Record each acknowledgement in the ledger, delete its spool file, and move the gateway's head once every range of the newest snapshot is confirmed. On iOS each finished upload is reported back through `settle`, from the delegate the system relaunches the app into.

A pass stops for one of a small set of reasons, and **`DEADLINE` is not an error and does not say so**: a window that ran out with bytes left is the ordinary case for a first backup of a camera roll, and a phone that reported it as a failure would train members to distrust a product that is working. `UNREACHABLE` is a gateway that did not answer; nothing is lost and the next pass resumes. `UNTRUSTED` is a machine at the gateway's address that is not the pinned gateway: the pass stops, takes nothing from it, and the status says so rather than calling the gateway absent. `MOVED` is a vault another phone claimed: this one freezes read-only. A pass learns it at contact, before it writes anything — it reads the writer epoch off the gateway's head — so a phone whose next pass has nothing to send freezes all the same rather than reading as alive. The member-facing sentences for each are `DrainCopy`'s ([`mobile/shared/.../sync/DrainCopy.kt`](../mobile/shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/dev/centraid/shared/sync/DrainCopy.kt)), not sentences this document re-spells.

**Acknowledgement is the `PUT`'s success** ([R-1080-7](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)), and the ledger is reconciled against every gateway's `exists` answer on every launch — the first pass of a core's life asks the gateway it reaches about every name the ledger confirms there, sends again whatever the gateway lost, and retakes a head the gateway no longer holds whole — so it is a cache of the gateways' truth and cannot drift. The backup line on Home and the Backup screen read the core's backup status — when the last snapshot was taken, when a gateway last acknowledged the head, how many content items are confirmed of how many (a file is confirmed once a gateway holds its bytes and an acknowledged snapshot holds its row: ordinary passes move bytes at once and take a snapshot at most hourly, so a file waits for its row in between), and what waits and why — and **never say more than a gateway acknowledged** ([`BackupLines`](../mobile/shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/dev/centraid/shared/sync/BackupLines.kt)).

**When a pass runs.** Every pass names its reason (`WakeReason`): the session opening, the app becoming active, a commit or a camera-roll import (debounced), the link changing, a window the operating system granted, **Back up now**, the app leaving the screen, and a restore finishing; the last three force a snapshot. **The session opening, the app becoming active and Back up now first walk the camera roll to its end**, so a photograph taken while Centraid was closed is in the vault, and in that pass, without the member opening Photos ([`ShelfDrain.installWalk`](../mobile/shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/dev/centraid/shared/sync/ShelfDrain.kt), R-1029-PH-4); a library change while the app is open and a photo-access grant walk to the end as well, and the background windows and the grace do not walk. A foreground pass survives backgrounding by finishing its current part under the platform's grace. The background windows are registered at launch on both platforms — iOS's processing task asks for external power and a network, and is asked for again at every background entry and after every pass; Android's are periodic WorkManager windows under constraints derived from the member's rule, one on a charger and an unmetered link for the long work, and an expedited one-off after a capture ([`BackgroundWindows`](../mobile/shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/dev/centraid/shared/sync/BackgroundWindows.kt)). Platform timing is opportunistic; correctness comes from the ledger and the spool.

### The member's transfer rule

**The member's transfer rule is the pass's input, and it is the member's own** ([D-1025-S7-60](decisions.md#sync-replicas-and-the-byte-plane)). The gateway does not know which SIM is in the phone, whether the member is abroad or what they agreed to, so the phone decides what it pays for. One rule per **device** — what it governs is a data plan, and a phone has one however many vaults it holds — kept in the shell's secure store (`TransferRule`, `mobile/shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/dev/centraid/shared/sync/TransferRule.kt`) and read before every pass, with the platform's answers to "is this link metered" and "is the phone charging" beside it — never cached across passes.

| rule | on Wi-Fi | on a metered link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `wifi-only` (**default**) | every original | no originals |
| `wifi-and-cellular-photos` | every original | a photograph's original; **a video's never** |
| `manual` | no originals | no originals |

Thumbnails and previews always cross, on any link and ahead of everything, because they are kilobytes and they are the grid. **A snapshot is not taken on a metered link** unless the rule lets photographs cross cellular or the member tapped Back up now ([R-1080-C38](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway): the records are a few megabytes, and a tap is the member asking). Only the originals are a member's decision, which is why the enum has no other cases. **Videos never on cellular is a fixed rule**, not a fourth setting: the two differ by three orders of magnitude, and a member who said "photos on cellular" did not say "a 900 MB video on cellular". **Include videos** is the member's other control, on the Backup screen: off, no video's original is backed up at all. `manual` withholds originals on every link, which is not the same as Wi-Fi off. **No shell computes any part of the table**: the core decides every item, and the same decision travels with each part the iOS background session carries. The Home header's rule sheet and the Backup screen draw the same sentences and write the same key ([R-1080-E9](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)).

### No push wake

**There is no push wake, and that is a recorded non-goal** ([D-1025-S7-41](decisions.md#deliberate-non-goals), and out of [#1080](https://github.com/srikanth235/centraid/issues/1080)'s scope). `WakeReason` has no push case: what is missing is a way to deliver a wake that does not put a third party between a member and their own vault. A phone that is not in the foreground backs up in the next window its platform grants, and on iOS the uploads it already handed off move without one.

## Byte budget and low disk

**The spool is bounded**: at most 2 GiB or a tenth of the free space, whichever is smaller. A part is sealed into it only when it fits what is left — one part larger than the whole budget goes alone into an empty spool — and preparing resumes once moving has drained it, so a backlog never fills the phone. The bound is on what waits, not on what can back up: an original larger than the room is sealed a window at a time, each window moved before the next is sealed ([R-1080-C39](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)), never half-sealed and never skipped. **The content store evicts nothing a gateway has not confirmed**: an original this phone owns may be evicted only once a gateway acknowledged it and it is not in a kept album, and an original fetched back from a gateway is a cache. Out of room is a wait, never a wipe: the vault's rows and the member's own bytes are never evicted to manufacture space.

## Performance guardrails

The absolute targets for a phone are in [`tests/journeys.json`](../tests/journeys.json), stated on the two reference devices ([glossary.md](glossary.md#hosts-and-clients)). A bounded read costs what it returns, and every read states its window: there is no unpaged read, and a whole-set read walks its pages and fails at its stated bound rather than returning a short list that reads as complete (`read_pages` in `crates/apps/kit/src/reads.rs`). A snapshot is hashed and sealed off the request path; the only step that holds the vault is the page copy, which cannot interleave with a commit ([R-1080-B6](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)).

## The per-state promise — the backup on iOS

What Centraid may tell a member about when their data will be safe, per app state. **The transport is settled and the timing is measured, not promised.** HTTPS to the member's own gateway is what lets iOS carry the bytes while the app is closed ([R-1080-2](decisions.md#backup-and-the-gateway)): the pass prepares sealed files while the app is alive, the operating system uploads them while it is suspended, and the app settles when it wakes. The sentences a member reads are `DrainCopy`'s and `BackupLines`', not this table's.

| App state | What Centraid does |
| --- | --- |
| **Open, on screen** | Passes run at open, on resume, after a commit or an import, and when the link changes; each part moves inside the app. The backup line shows what is confirmed and what waits. |
| **Leaving the screen** | A pass takes a snapshot and finishes its current part under the platform's grace, then hands the prepared spool to the background session. |
| **Suspended, iOS** | **The operating system uploads the handed-off parts** while Centraid is not running, each under its own cellular allowance, and relaunches the app to settle them. The processing window — on a charger, with a network — prepares and seals the next batch; a refresh window settles. Progress is the ledger and the spool, never the task. |
| **Backgrounded, Android** | The periodic and charging windows run the whole pass in the process, under constraints derived from the member's rule; **Back up now** is a user-initiated data transfer job on API 34 and later and a `dataSync` foreground service below it, with a notification. |
| **Force-quit, iOS** | The platform cancels the app's background uploads and grants no windows until Centraid is opened again. Nothing is lost: the spool and the ledger resume from where they stopped (`DrainCopy.FORCE_QUIT_SENTENCE`). |
| **Low Power Mode, or Background App Refresh off** | The platform pauses background work, so nothing new is prepared until Centraid is opened. |
| **A gateway asleep or out of reach** | The pass stops `UNREACHABLE` — whether the gateway was out of reach when the pass began or went part-way through it, mid-upload or mid-question; what waits is counted as waiting for the gateway, with when it was last seen, and the next pass resumes from the same place, sending nothing the gateway acknowledged. A laptop that sleeps is a gateway that is waited for, not a failure. |
| **On a metered link** | No snapshot unless the member taps Back up now, and no original the rule withholds; thumbnails and previews still move. What waits is counted as waiting for Wi-Fi. |
| **An original only in iCloud** | Counted as waiting for iCloud; downloaded to be backed up only under the rule and inside a window. |
| **Everything confirmed** | Idle. "Backed up" with no qualifier needs the records acknowledged **and** every content item confirmed. |
| **Photo access denied** | The camera-roll walk is idle; **the grid is unaffected**, because it reads the vault and not the camera roll. |

**What is not yet known is how much gets through**, which the platform's scheduling decides and which a measurement on a **named reference device** settles — never a simulator ([R-1020-20](decisions.md#governance-and-the-pr-gate)). [`mobile/maestro/backup-measurement.md`](../mobile/maestro/backup-measurement.md) is the protocol, with three claims and fixed thresholds: the first backup of a 2,000-asset library finishes in one evening; a night's photographs are confirmed by morning with the app closed on iOS; a fresh phone shows the whole grid within minutes of the words. A missed threshold is reported as missed, never moved.

**HEIC originals upload untouched**, as the camera wrote them; their thumbnails and previews are rendered on the phone by the platform's own decoder and staged as derivatives of the original, so an iPhone's grid no longer falls back to a multi-megabyte original ([docs/photos/README.md](photos/README.md#derivatives)).
